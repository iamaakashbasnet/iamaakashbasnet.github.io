Good product engineering is mostly invisible. Users feel speed and clarity. Teams feel whether the codebase, and the product, still make sense six months later.

I work across the stack with a bias toward clarity: solid contracts between services, typed boundaries where they earn their keep, and tools that fail loudly in the right places. The same discipline applies to product decisions: what ships, what waits, and what never earns a surface.

## What I optimize for

**Architecture** that can grow without becoming a junk drawer. Shared patterns when they help, not when they hurt.

**Performance** as a product feature, not a late audit. Clearer data boundaries, fewer accidental layers, less complexity for its own sake.

**API collaboration** that treats contracts as first-class. Strong typing on both sides of the wire beats heroic debugging.

## Minimalism is a constraint

Minimalism is not empty space for its own sake. It is removing anything that does not earn attention, so the thing that matters can.

That is the same instinct I bring to CovertView: fewer surfaces, sharper intent, systems you can trust under load.
