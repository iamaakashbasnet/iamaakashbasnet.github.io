Steve Jobs left a product philosophy in two parts: what the thing should be, and how it should be made. Not a process. A set of standards.

## What the product should be

### Intersection

**Technology alone is not enough.**

Jobs said the best products live at the crossroads of technology and the liberal arts. Engineering married to humanistic thinking. Science without soul, he believed, produces tools nobody loves.

### Simplicity

**Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication.**

For Jobs, stripping everything that doesn't need to be there wasn't laziness. It was the hardest, most disciplined form of design. A product should do fewer things, and do them perfectly.

### Emotion

**Products must have a soul.**

A device isn't just hardware and software. Jobs insisted it carry a feeling: the weight of it, the sound it makes, what it says about you. Taste and emotional resonance were product features.

### Integration

**Own the whole stack.**

When you control hardware and software end to end, Jobs argued, you can make the experience seamless. Fragmentation was the enemy of great design.

### Focus

**Decide what not to do.**

Innovation, for Jobs, was saying no to a thousand things. A focused product line beats a sprawling one. When he returned to Apple in 1997, he cut about 70% of the products. That restraint saved the company.

## How the product should be made

### Design

**Design is how it works.**

Most people think design is how something looks. Jobs believed design is how it works. Every interaction, every flow, every transition is a design decision, not just the surface.

### Craft

**Care about the back of the fence.**

His father taught him that a good craftsman uses fine wood on the back of a cabinet too, even if no one sees it. Jobs demanded the same of Apple's internals, code, and packaging.

### Vision

**Don't ask customers what they want.**

Customers can't imagine what doesn't exist yet. Jobs's job, as he saw it, was to show people a future they didn't know they needed, then make them wonder how they lived without it.

### Perfectionism

**Real artists ship, but don't ship garbage.**

Perfectionism without execution is delusion. Jobs held both truths: ship when it's ready, but "ready" is a high bar. Shipping mediocrity is worse than waiting for excellence.

### Narrative

**The product launch is part of the product.**

How a product is revealed, named, packaged, and talked about was, for Jobs, inseparable from the product itself. The story you tell shapes the experience people have with the thing.
